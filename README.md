\# Nike Consumer Direct Offense: Forensic BI Analysis

\### How a D2C Pivot Led to a $5.6B Revenue Shortfall | FY2015–FY2024



\---



\## Project Overview



This project is a forensic business intelligence analysis of Nike's 

Consumer Direct Offense (CDO) strategy — a bold move announced in 2017 

where Nike decided to cut over 50,000 wholesale retail partners and sell 

directly to consumers through its own stores, website and apps.



On paper the strategy made sense. In reality, it led to operating margin 

compression, a $9.7B inventory crisis, an 8-point market share loss to 

challenger brands like Hoka and On Running, and a stock decline of nearly 

60% from its peak.



This analysis uses real publicly available financial data to understand 

exactly what went wrong — and what a more balanced approach might have 

looked like.

\## Key Findings



\- Operating margin fell \*\*354 basis points\*\* from its FY2021 peak of 

&#x20; 15.58% to 12.29% in FY2024 — despite D2C share rising to 44%

\- Nike's SG\&A costs rose from 30.75% to 32.27% of revenue — 

&#x20; consuming all gross margin gains from D2C

\- Inventory peaked at \*\*$9.7 billion\*\* in Q4 FY2022, forcing heavy 

&#x20; discounting that damaged brand premium

\- Nike's athletic footwear market share dropped from \*\*38.2% to 30.2%\*\* 

&#x20; while Hoka grew from 0.8% to 7.4% and On Running from 0.3% to 6.8%

\- Wholesale channels show LTV:CAC ratios of \*\*24x+\*\* vs 6.5x for D2C 

&#x20; digital — challenging the core assumption behind CDO

\- Under a balanced 60/40 wholesale-to-D2C scenario, estimated revenue 

&#x20; in FY2024 would have been approximately \*\*$5.6B higher\*\*
